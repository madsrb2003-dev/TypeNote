# Local PDF corpus review

Reviewed on 2026-10-22 for the PDF-to-editable-score feasibility milestone. This is a source-quality and notation review, not a recognition-accuracy result.

## Scope and method

The private `sample_scores/` folder contains 15 PDFs, 61 PDF pages, and 23,323,870 bytes in total. Poppler `pdfinfo`, `pdfimages -list`, `pdffonts`, and first-page `pdftotext` were run locally against every file. All 15 files report no encryption. Page sizes vary; they are not uniformly A4.

Thirty-one pages were rendered locally with `pdftoppm` at a maximum dimension of 1500 pixels and visually inspected: the first page of every file, one additional page of every file, and one further page of *Cantina Band*. The exact inspected pages are listed below. The first page of *Cantina Band* is a cover; its first music page is PDF page 2. Page numbers here always refer to the PDF's one-based page index, not a printed book page or measure number.

Classification combines image/font inventory with visible paper texture, page curvature, skew, and notation edges. Lack of extracted text alone does not establish scanning. Embedded font/text data is not a structured musical score. No PDF or extracted page was uploaded. Page images and raw inspection output are temporary local material under ignored `test-results/omr-review/`; this document contains no reproduced score images, note transcriptions, purchaser details, or handwritten personal messages.

## Implications for a recognition trial

1. Separate source-quality strata in results: vector input, clean raster, paper scan, and low-resolution raster. The 15 files do not constitute 15 independent engraving styles or a representative population of all sheet music; several share publishers and scan conditions. Report per-file results as well as pooled totals.
2. Select musical pages explicitly. A no-staff cover is not an OMR failure. Preserve its place in source-page numbering, and retain sparse pages such as the final page of *Flight of The Pigeon*.
3. Keep the original PDF immutable. Evaluate normalized raster input as a separate experimental condition; do not overwrite evidence or infer better resolution from resampling. Deskew alone will not straighten the book curvature visible in *Corynorhinus* and *Severus and Lily*.
4. Prioritize musical reconstruction beyond notehead detection: pitch spelling and accidental scope, onset/duration, voice and staff assignment, rests, measure capacity, chord grouping, clef/meter changes, ties versus slurs, tuplets and repeats. Fingerings, pedal, octave and expression marks need separate coverage checks.
5. Identify uncertain source glyphs and handwriting explicitly. In particular, do not count an unverified time-signature guess for *Kadoran* as accurate ground truth. Handwriting may be an intended performance annotation, but recognizing it as a printed note or dynamic is a separate error category.
6. Measure correction effort on the existing full-MusicXML editing path. Successful XML generation or readable re-engraving alone is not proof that notation was recognized correctly. Count missing, inserted and substituted events and marks against a manually checked reference, and record time spent correcting them.

A practical first hand-audit set is *I Giorni* page 1 (clean sparse notation), *Cantina Band* page 2 (chromatic chords), *Lily's Theme* pages 1-2 (tuplets/pedal), *Corynorhinus* page 2 (curvature and meter/clef changes), *Flight of The Pigeon* page 1 (handwriting), and *The Imitation Game* page 3 (low resolution). The locations above extend coverage to every score without initially transcribing all 61 pages.

## Initial hand reference

A separate ignored `test-results/omr-review/hand-audit-reference.json` records a manually read reference for four bars: *A Lannister Always Pays His Debts* page 1, measure 1 lower staff; *La dispute* page 1, measures 1-2 upper staff; and *Cantina Band* page 2, measure 1 upper staff. These three original pages were rendered again at 180 dpi, and enlarged local crops were inspected to verify staff positions, durations and markings independently of recognition output. The reference contains 12 written note/chord events and 20 pitch instances, with rational onsets/durations and separate marking counts. No score transcription is included in tracked documentation. This small sample supports a first error count only, not a corpus-wide accuracy estimate.

## Verification limits

All metadata commands completed successfully, and all 31 selected page renders were inspected. This source review and small hand reference did not run an OMR provider, compute pitch/rhythm accuracy, time human corrections, or establish the exact intended meaning of ambiguous source glyphs. Provider trials and comparisons, if run, are recorded separately. Thirty of the 61 PDF pages were not visually inspected; notation inventories are therefore observed examples, not exhaustive file-level coverage. The corpus contains copyrighted and personalized material and is suitable for this local investigation; no redistribution or public test-fixture rights were established.
